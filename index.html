TNTZO (Ti-34Nb-13Ta-5Zr-0.3O) is a β-titanium alloy with a Young’s modulus of 56.5 GPa, about half that of Ti-6Al-4V.

The final stem, FV6, is fully dimensioned on its A4 engineering drawing (PS2-42722-FV6-001): front and side views, two sections at 2:1, material, lattice and manufacturing notes.

The models are the CAD geometry analysed in SOLIDWORKS Simulation, at full size and lightly simplified for the web. The prints on the poster are 80% scale, in PLA, and check geometry only. Stresses come from an ISO 7206-4 style test at 2300 N. Each stress plot uses the same 0 to 150 MPa scale as the poster.

Abstract as submitted

Stress shielding remains a major challenge in cementless total hip arthroplasty, arising from the stiffness mismatch between conventional metallic femoral stems and surrounding bone. This study investigated whether combining low-modulus β-titanium (TNTZO) with stress-informed lattice optimisation could improve proximal femoral load transfer while maintaining structural integrity.

A systematic finite element analysis (FEA) framework was implemented in SOLIDWORKS to optimise blade thickness, neck diameter, material reduction strategies and lattice distribution. Design variants were evaluated under an ISO 7206-4-inspired loading configuration of 2300 N, using regional von Mises stress, construct stiffness and an estimated fatigue safety factor as performance metrics. Physiological FEA under a 1619 N load subsequently compared graded, uniform and solid femoral stem configurations using TNTZO and Ti-6Al-4V, assessing stress shielding across the seven Gruen zones.

The optimised stem incorporated a 61-pore load-informed graded lattice, a patient-matched calcar collar and a reinforced neck geometry. The final configuration achieved a 10.1% material volume reduction and 6% lower construct stiffness relative to the solid stem, while maintaining an estimated fatigue safety factor of 1.66. TNTZO reduced the calcar stress-shielding index from approximately 65% for Ti-6Al-4V to 48%. However, graded and uniform lattice configurations demonstrated nearly identical proximal load-transfer behaviour, indicating that material modulus exerted a substantially greater influence on stress shielding than local porosity distribution.

These findings highlight the importance of material–geometry interactions in orthopaedic implant optimisation. Although stress-informed lattice placement enabled material reduction within the assumed fatigue constraints, low-modulus alloy selection was the dominant factor influencing proximal load transfer. Further experimental fatigue characterisation and implant–bone interface evaluation are required to establish clinical applicability.

Rechecked since submission: the final stem (FV6) is 9.0% lighter and 6.4% less stiff than its solid twin. The 10.1% and 6% figures are the stage 4 lattice against the solid stem.

Limits and next steps

  • Stresses are comparative. The ISO runs used β = 0° (the standard uses 9°); a β = 9° check more than doubled body stress. Next: re-run at β = 9°.
  • Material data are borrowed. 170 MPa is for aged TNT5Zr (Kong et al., 2022), and ageing stiffens TNTZO to about 80 GPa (Ibrahim et al., 2024), which would shrink the bone benefit. Next: fatigue-test LPBF TNTZO.
  • Models are simplified: linear, static, bonded; one femur, one load case; a 3.17 mm lattice for the 1.2 mm production cell. Next: press-fit micromotion, gait and stair loads, an LPBF build with micro-CT.
